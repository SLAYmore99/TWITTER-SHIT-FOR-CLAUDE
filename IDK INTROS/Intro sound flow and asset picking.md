# Intro sound flow and asset picking

Rules for high-pace intros and video essays, learned from Reference 1 (the "new renaissance" essay). Evidence and charts: [[YouTube intro references breakdown]]. Chart: `INTROS\_analysis\sound_flow_map.png`.

## 1. The music rule
**Switch the music when the tempo or the context changes.** One context gets one piece of music, for as long as the context lasts. This is Rachit's read of the video, and the measurements agree with it.

## 3. Five ways he joins one piece of music to the next
1. **Riser + hit (16.2 s).** The low end rises about 14 dB over 2 s into the cut; the hit leads the picture by about 0.17 s. Use for a chapter title.
2. **Hard stop + slam (46.35 s).** The old music ends dead, there is a half-second dip, and the new music enters at full level with the reveal. Used once in 90 s. Use for the biggest turn.
3. **Borrowed audio as the bridge (13-16 s).** Drop the music while a found clip plays with its own sound, and start a riser under the clip's last seconds.
4. **Seamless entry (22.4 s).** The new pattern enters with no level change. Use when the shift is gentle, the next beat of the same story.
5. **Texture stop (72.8 s).** The pulses simply stop and one thin note stays. Use for an emotional pivot.

## 4. Deciding when to switch (checklist for your own script)
1. Mark the contexts in the script (hook, problem, history, turn, now, argument, close). Each new context is a candidate switch.
2. For each one, ask what pulse and mood it needs: calm explaining, driven montage, emotional close. If the next context needs a different tempo or mood, switch. If not, keep the music.
3. Pick the join by importance: biggest reveal = hard stop + slam; chapter title = riser + hit; found clip = borrowed audio; gentle shift = seamless entry; emotional pivot = texture stop.
4. Land the first strong beat of the new music on the cut (standard editing practice, not something I measured).
5. Keep the voice level constant and duck the music under it.
6. Pieces last as long as their context: about 25 s for the main story stretches, 3-7 s in the hook where contexts change fast. That is about 6-8 pieces in 90 s.

### In Premiere
- Use two music tracks (A2 and A3) and alternate between them, so a join can overlap or leave a gap.
- Riser SFX on its own track, ending 2-5 frames before the cut. Impact on its own track, at the cut or 2-4 frames before it.
- For a hard stop, mute the old music (do not just duck it), and leave a 3-5 frame gap in the voice if he pauses there.
- Voice on the Dialogue type in Essential Sound, with ducking against the music, so the voice level never changes.

## 5. Picking assets
Test every candidate with five questions:
1. **Recognised in time?** A flash (under 0.3 s) must be an icon you read in 5-6 frames. A normal shot gets 1 s. If it needs 2 s to understand, it cannot be a flash.
2. **Metaphor, not echo?** It should stand for the idea, not repeat the word.
3. **Different class from its neighbours?** Never two shots of the same kind in a row.
4. **Right register for the chapter?** History is engravings, paintings, B&W, cream paper. Now is saturated screens, news and social.
5. **Room for the text?** Pick images with empty space, or a subject you can place words around.

Classes used in Ref 1: found TV or news, archive film, painting, engraving, screen or UI capture, product close-up, stock cinematic, cartoon or meme, graphic overlay on the talking head.

| Spoken idea | Asset in Ref 1 | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| "The 2030s" | Earthrise on black | One image for the whole future; huge negative space for the date |
| "will be the start" | Finger pressing a record button, close-up | A physical "start", and a texture change from the face |
| "renaissance" (eras of invention) | Rocket, B&W stunt film, early Mac, Apple keynote, iPod in 0.08-0.25 s each | Five eras in 1.5 s, each an icon, each a different texture |
| "AI is changing everything" | Wall of headlines, 2 frames each | The flood is the point; nobody reads them |
| "this guy" | Portrait with a hand-drawn red arrow | A pointing gesture, so the face is the answer |
| "30,000 books turned into 200,000,000" | Counter and book icons over the talking head | Numbers need a graphic, and he stays the anchor |
| "spread across Europe" | Old map with pins dropping | The idea literally happens on screen |
| "content pumped out in a second" | Spam factory conveyor | A factory line says mass production; also a joke |
| "handmade" over machine-made | Cartoon clip about a handmade gift | Humour on an opinion line, not on a fact |
| "of effort" | Michelangelo's hands touching, then a green wireframe hand | Old and new in one gesture |

Two habits to copy: jokes and pop culture only on **opinion** lines, never on facts; and the creator's own footage stays the home base, so the wild cutaways always return to a face.

**Rights.** Ref 1 uses news clips, cartoons, ads and film. While this is practice only, any footage is fine; before anything is published, use only licensed, public-domain or your own footage (see [[Workflow rules]]). Substitutes: public-domain paintings and engravings (Met Open Access, Rijksmuseum, Wikimedia Commons, Library of Congress), NASA media, Internet Archive film, Pexels, Pixabay, Mixkit, Adobe Stock, and graphics you build.
